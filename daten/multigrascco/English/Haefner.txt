Course
Mr. Turner presented for a planned colostomy reversal due to previous unclear conglomerate tumor at the rectosigmoid junction, most likely inflammatory in origin, with sealed perforation and localized peritonitis on 11/19/2023. Colonoscopy did not reveal any evidence of malignancy, so we proceeded with the planned surgery on 11/19/2023.

During surgery, only a 2.5-centimeter rectal stump was found, so creating an anastomosis would have resulted in fecal incontinence for the patient. Therefore, the reconnection was not performed, and a new colostomy was created.

Postoperative recovery was prolonged due to slow return of bowel function. Symptomatic postoperative anemia was successfully treated with the transfusion of two units of packed red blood cells. The transfusions were without complications. Due to severe abdominal pain, a CT scan of the abdomen was performed on 11/21/2023, which, in addition to the expected postoperative amounts of free air and fluid, also revealed a significant subcutaneous parastomal hematoma. Since, over the course, the colostomy increasingly passed stool and ultrasound showed the hematoma was regressing, no surgical revision was necessary. Wound healing was unremarkable, and the staples were removed on schedule.

Further recovery involved very slow mobilization.
Incidental laboratory findings showed increasing thrombocytosis, so thrombosis prophylaxis with 100 mg of acetylsalicylic acid was initiated.
On 12/03/23, Mr. Turner was able to be discharged from our inpatient care. If you have any further questions, please feel free to contact us at any time.
