I am writing to update you regarding our mutual patient, who was admitted to our inpatient care from 11/02 to 11/24/28.

Rehabilitation interventions performed:

Activating nursing care, physical therapy, occupational therapy, electrotherapy (interference current), massages, local heat application, and psychological counseling.

Rehabilitation Course and Special Issues: Measures were taken for general strengthening, reduction of pain, increasing muscular strength, and training of memory functions. The rehabilitation treatment was significantly complicated, as Ms. Mitchell, due to advanced dementia, was unfortunately no longer able to reliably perform even simple movement sequences independently, even with intensive and repeated training. Besides the marked memory impairment, severe disturbances in spatial orientation also complicated therapy. During the course of the inpatient stay, a slight improvement in mobility was achieved. At latest, she had secure, independent sitting and standing balance. She also practiced walking outdoors on various surfaces and walking up and down slopes. With a rolling walker, she was able to walk over 100 meters. Climbing two flights of stairs (using the railing), both up and down, was possible using alternating and step-to-step methods. Washing, showering, personal hygiene, and dressing (except for donning compression stockings and correctly applying the lumbar brace) could only be carried out slowly and independently under guidance up until the last. All transfers were adequately safe when done alone. Toilet use, both during the day and at night, was possible independently. Regarding the presence of low back pain due to severe degenerative changes and profound osteoporotic changes of the lower axial skeleton, minor improvements were achieved in the interim. The use of the morphine analog (fentanyl [Durogesic transdermal therapeutic system] in combination with a peripherally acting analgesic (metamizole [Novaminsulfon 500])) resulted in satisfactory pain reduction. None of the physical therapy modalities applied produced outstanding improvement on their own. The corset brought by the patient could not be safely managed due to the closure mechanism, so we prescribed a lumbar orthosis with a velcro fastener. Back-sparing behavior was practiced, but could not be consistently or independently reproduced.
Anticoagulation was managed with phenprocoumon (Coumadin); please refer to the enclosed copy of the Coumadin card for exact dosage. Due to concurrent chronic heart failure, chronic renal insufficiency, and a tendency toward dehydration, frequent monitoring and adjustment of diuretic medication were required.
Given the patient’s lower leg edema in the context of varicose veins and heart failure, we prescribed anti-embolism stockings. No change in antihypertensive medication was necessary as her blood pressure readings remained unremarkable. Intermittent pain in the tendons at the chiasma tendinum crurale was successfully treated with diclofenac cream compresses (Voltaren Emulgel).
Because of her known dementia, Ms. Mitchell was also followed by our psychology staff. Neuropsychological testing revealed deficits in all domains examined: Short- and medium-term memory is hugely impaired—retention falls to nearly zero after just a few minutes. Cognitive speed is reduced, abstract and logical thinking are diminished. Executive planning and spatial abilities are also affected. The patient is disoriented regarding time and partially regarding place. Her remote memory is also uncertain. Overall, findings are consistent with a pattern of advanced dementia of several years’ duration. Because the patient cannot grasp even simple contexts and experiences severe memory deficits, she requires substantial support in her daily life, especially for new or more complex activities.

Assistive devices prescribed for post-acute care: toilet seat riser, bathtub board with handle, reaching aid, Lumbamed plus lumbar orthosis. The following aspects of social medicine have been applied for: counseling regarding disability rights, although Ms. Mitchell currently refuses to make a formal application. Her husband and daughter have a notarized power of attorney for the patient, and there is also a living will.
