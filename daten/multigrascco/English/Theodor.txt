Medical details:
The patient was cardiopulmonarily stable, with no feverish infections. During his inpatient stay, the patient reported sensory disturbances in his left arm and, later on, his left leg. On 11/15/2021, he was found to have weakness of the left biceps and triceps brachii muscles with decreased biceps tendon reflex on the left, a finding that contrasted sharply with admission results. This resolved quickly and was interpreted as fatigue resulting from therapy. The disturbance later resolved and was no longer detectable at discharge. In several thorough medical consultations, Mr. Peterson took the opportunity to learn extensively about multiple sclerosis and options for relapse prevention. In light of the over four-year relatively slowly progressive course, we have decided for the time being not to initiate relapse-preventive medication.

Physical therapy
Truncal and lower-extremity ataxia was present, with hypotonic abdominal musculature and decreased reciprocal innervation of leg muscles bilaterally. Balance reactions while standing and walking were reduced, and with prolonged walking over an extended period, the patient complained of increasing left foot drop. The physical therapy focused on conditioning, muscle stretching, and coordination training for trunk ataxia.

Mr. Peterson participated in a fine motor skills group and received sensorimotor-cognitive one-on-one training.
