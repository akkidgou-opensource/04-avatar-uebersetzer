We are writing to inform you about our mutual patient, Emily Norris, * 04/19/2020, who was seen on 02/09/22 for outpatient care at our facility.

Initial Presentation:
Ms. Norris presents with spina bifida at approximately the L5 vertebral level. She has not undergone any surgical procedures to date. She is fitted with two dorsally-applied, below-the-knee length plastic orthoses with anterior hook-and-loop fasteners, open-ring design without ankle support.

Findings:
Healed, non-irritated closure scar, pronounced, flexible left-convex thoracolumbar scoliosis, no significant kyphosis.
Standing evaluation reveals good stabilization of knees and hips with nearly full extension, but marked bilateral planovalgus foot deformity.
Clinical assessment shows full passive hip range of motion bilaterally in all directions, unrestricted, with full knee flexion/extension. Both feet display a calcaneal valgus deformity with pes planus; the forefoot is correctible into pronation. Sensation appears retained to the plantar aspect of the foot (patient description limited). Right tibial extension strength at 2–3, with visible activation of toe extensors and slight contraction of tibialis anterior muscle producing minimal dorsal foot excursion; left side shows activation of toe extensors only, with no palpable tibialis anterior activity. All toes are in a flexed position but correctible; plantarflexion is mainly accomplished via the toe flexors, but only at strength grade 3.
Active knee extension is strength grade 4- bilaterally, flexion is 4. Hip flexion is 4 bilaterally, extension is 3–4. Abduction strength is 4-, adduction is 4. Thus, there is good muscular activity in all areas except the feet. The patient stands fairly well aligned across all joints.
X-ray: Rippstein I: Good delineation of the femoral head epiphyses, minimal lateralization, more pronounced on the left than right, acetabular roofs still overlapping, Shenton-Menard line not significantly interrupted, acetabular angle left 30°, right also just under 30°.
Diagnosis:
Spina bifida with left L4 paralysis level, right L4/L5, bilateral planovalgus-calcaneal foot deformities, minimal, pronounced left-convex total scoliosis.

Plan:
It is important to ensure hip stability by physical therapy interventions and strengthening exercises for the abductors and extensors of both hips. Internal stabilization is currently preferred, and abduction bracing is not indicated at this time. Instead, centering the hip through abductor strengthening is more crucial. For the feet, the orthotic management described above is indicated. The parents will consider the course of action with the Orthotic and Prosthetic Center after receiving the cost estimate.
