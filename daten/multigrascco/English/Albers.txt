Ms. Chambers is known psychiatrically for delusional ideation. She amputated her own left hand at home and set her scalp hair on fire. She called a friend, who immediately notified EMS. According to protocol, the patient opened the door herself and was holding the severed hand. She was promptly transported to the emergency department of our center. Upon arrival, clinical and radiological findings showed a complete amputation of the left hand. Both ears and especially the right side of the neck were burned to the second and third degree. Head CT did not reveal evidence of acute intracranial trauma; prior surgeries included osteoplastic trepanation of the left posterior cranial fossa and right frontotemporal region as well as vascular clips left infratentorially near the left vertebral artery and right middle cerebral artery.

Course of Treatment
Ms. Chambers was operated on immediately by the team from the Hand and Microsurgery Department. The hand was able to be replanted in an operation that lasted all night. A dorsal soft tissue defect was temporarily covered with a Vacuseal. The burned areas were initially treated with antiseptic Lavasept-gel dressings. Subsequently, Ms. Chambers was intubated and ventilated, and admitted to the Burn Intensive Care Unit.
On 03/25/2029, wound revision and Vacuseal change were performed. The head dressing was changed to enzymatic debridement with Iruxol ointment. The first debriding procedure took place on 03/27/2029.
A further debridement was done for mostly clean wounds on the head on 04/06/2029. On 04/08/2029, the head defects were covered with keratinocyte spray suspension and split-thickness skin grafts taken from the left thigh. Graft take was uneventful. Only in the right retroauricular neck and in small areas on the head did further grafts become necessary on 04/23/2029. The head and face are currently healed except for minor defects. All donor sites are fully re-epithelialized. Multiple areas of alopecia remain in the hair-bearing scalp, corresponding to areas of initial third-degree burns.

On 04/23/2029, the granulating defect on the dorsum of the left hand was also covered with a split-thickness skin graft.
The temporary arthrodesis in the wrist was removed on 05/06/2029. Ongoing physical and occupational therapy were provided.

Psychiatric Course
Per history from her friend and her outpatient psychiatrist and psychotherapist, the patient had been acutely psychotic since the beginning of the year. She had stopped her maintenance dose of 2 mg of her antipsychotic; the treating psychiatrist Dr. Dr. Katherine Morin was aware. Despite frequent telephone contact with the treating psychiatrist, she could not be persuaded to restart her medication. On the day of her self-injury, her concerned friend accompanied her to the community psychiatric service. As Ms. Chambers did not agree to voluntary inpatient admission and there were no grounds for involuntary admission, a visit to the outpatient psychiatrist was planned. On the way, Ms. Chambers jumped out of the taxi and inflicted the aforementioned injuries at home. She informed her friend by phone, who then called the fire department.
Initially, there were significant thought disturbances. She had delusions that she was God. She recalled cutting off her left hand with a bread knife, saying it was done to atone for evil in the world. At first, she was highly inappropriate, spitting continuously, and meaningful conversation was difficult. With sedation (Ativan) and, later, a switch to haloperidol, these symptoms decreased, and the antipsychotic was switched, in agreement with her, to risperidone 6 mg. She asked that Ativan be tapered.
With treatment, she became largely free of psychotic experiences, calm, able to engage, and expressed regret for her actions. After initially refusing psychiatric inpatient transfer, she finally agreed after a motivational interview to transfer voluntarily to your department. She showed a strong interest in therapy and was knowledgeable about her medications, including receptor mechanisms, from her past experience. Affectively, she remained somewhat emotionally distant from the gravity of her self-injury.
The purpose of transfer is to further increase her resilience by gradually reintroducing her to mild external stimuli following intensive inpatient care. Additionally, community-based psychiatric follow-up in an outpatient setting is being sought after this serious self-injury, as well as ongoing therapeutic support to help her adjust to the consequences in daily life.
