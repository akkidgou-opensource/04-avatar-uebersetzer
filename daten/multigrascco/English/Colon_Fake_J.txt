Course
Admission labs reveal elevated inflammatory markers and abdominal x-ray shows an overdistended transverse colon with a loop width of 8.5 centimeters and nonspecific small intestine fluid levels. Given these findings, an abdominal and pelvic CT with contrast is performed.
A roughly 3 centimeter, suspicious, subtotal stenosis is seen in the distal sigmoid colon. Orally from this area, the colon is fluid-filled with extensive air-fluid levels.
Given the patient’s symptoms and all findings together, he is admitted to the hospital and surgery is scheduled for the following day. Follow-up abdominal x-ray shows, compared to prior, a trend toward progression with increasing colonic gas and more air-fluid levels. Thus,
the indication is made for emergency laparotomy, and the patient undergoes surgery on 11/04/2064 under uneventful general anesthesia, American Society of Anesthesiologists Physical Status 3. Afterward, the patient is transferred to the intensive care unit and on 11/18/64 is moved back to our regular ward. Histopathology of the surgical specimen shows a poorly differentiated invasive adenocarcinoma of the sigmoid colon with infiltration into mesocolic fat, blood, and lymphatic vessels, as well as metastases in 7 of 14 lymph nodes.
With these findings, the patient is presented at our multidisciplinary tumor board. IV antibiotic therapy with Tazonam started in the ICU is continued 3 times daily for 5 days. Antihypertensive therapy is also maintained. An internal medicine consult is obtained, as the patient reports recurrent episodes of irregular rapid heartbeat postoperatively. After a thorough internal exam, an episode of tachycardic atrial fibrillation is assumed, and once wound healing allows, oral anticoagulation with a vitamin K antagonist is indicated; alternatively, a low molecular weight heparin in 1.5 therapeutic dose once daily. Slow diet advancement is well tolerated by the patient, allowing parenteral nutrition to be stopped on 11/23/2064. Regular bowel management with Colonorm and Molaxole is started. Under this regimen, the patient has daily bowel movements. Additionally, the daily fixed analgesic regimen is switched to as-needed, under which the patient is pain-free. Regularly performed wound checks show good wound healing.
On 11/24/2064, part of the staples are removed and a secondary closure performed for minor wound dehiscence. Multiple times, stoma care is explained to the patient and also to his husband, so that on the above-mentioned date, the patient can be discharged in good age-appropriate condition, symptom-free, mobile, and afebrile.
