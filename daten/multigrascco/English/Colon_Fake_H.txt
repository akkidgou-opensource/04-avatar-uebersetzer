Clinical Course
The patient was initially admitted to our Stroke Unit due to the presenting symptoms described above.
No acute ischemic area could be identified on initial cranial CT.
Patient was started on IV Aspirin. Cranial MRI was then performed, revealing
a 2 cm acute ischemic lesion in the left precentral cortex.
The day after admission, blood was noted in the stool in context of known rectal neoplasm and
post-chemoradiation status. Aspirin therapy was withheld. Colonoscopy was ordered.

On 03/16/2023 symptoms progressed with worsening of dysarthria and
right upper limb paresis.
CT follow-up showed no interval change, but repeat MRI with intracranial angiography demonstrated increase in size of
the infarct area previously described on CT.
This correlated with the updated clinical presentation.

On 04/19/2023 the patient was transferred to our in-house neurorehabilitation unit. The therapies started on the
acute care unit were intensified, with the addition of occupational therapy sessions.

Colonoscopy showed the known rectal carcinoma to be vulnerable,
explaining the recent hematochezia. An upper abdominal and pelvic MRI were performed
the following day.
This confirmed the tumor finding, along with continued infiltration of perirectal fat,
and a suspicious lymph node measuring 7 mm in short axis.
No evidence of metastasis in chest CT. Referral made to surgical outpatient clinic and Pre-Admission Testing.
At the tumor board, decision was made to prepare the patient for abdominoperineal resection.
Surgery scheduled for 05/02/2023, with patient consent.
With ongoing physical and occupational therapy, patient recently able to walk
