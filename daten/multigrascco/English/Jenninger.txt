4. Risk factors:

History: For years, he has had known cardiac arrhythmias. In the past, this was partially attributed to alcohol abuse and diazepam abuse. For years, however, he has been sober and has taken fewer tranquilizers. The last long-term electrocardiogram performed by you on 09/02/18 showed a regular sinus rhythm, with ventricular rate fluctuations of a minimum of 55 and a maximum of 149 beats per minute, as well as occasional ventricular extrasystoles (77) and no supraventricular extrasystoles. Four pauses with a maximum pause length of 2.15 milliseconds due to temporary atrioventricular issues are denied. With exertion, he experiences shortness of breath, which has been attributed to his excess weight of 19 kilograms. No leg edema. Risk factors: ongoing nicotine abuse of 30 cigarettes a day.

Physical exam findings: 49-year-old patient, blood pressure at rest: right arm 140/80, left arm 120/80 mmHg. Weight 94 kilograms. Height 175 centimeters. No signs of cardiac insufficiency. No palpable apical beat. First and second heart sounds of normal intensity under beta-blocker therapy. No extrasystoles. No heart murmur. Lungs clear to auscultation. Peripheral pulses regular.

Chest X-ray in two views with barium swallow: Cardiac silhouette enlarged toward the left, with a CT ratio of 0.42 and clear retrocardiac space. Bilateral prominent hila. Aortic sclerosis noted. Lungs and sinuses without abnormal findings.

Resting electrocardiogram: Sinus rhythm at 75 beats per minute, left axis. Borderline normal PQ interval of 0.2 seconds and normal QT interval. Chest leads unremarkable.

Diagnostic exercise stress test ( / ): During bicycle ergometer stress test in a seated position, starting at 50 watts and increasing by 50 watts every minute up to 250 watts, there was an increase in pulse from 60 to a maximum of 130 beats per minute and a blood pressure increase from 112/74 up to a maximum of 179/70 mmHg. Test was stopped due to leg fatigue. No angina pectoris. No ST depression. On follow-up, 2 ventricular extrasystoles noted.

Color Doppler echocardiography: Left atrium enlarged at 45 millimeters and left ventricle with end-diastolic diameter of 61 and end-systolic diameter of 34 millimeters is borderline enlarged. Posterior wall thickened at 19 millimeters and with normal motion. Septum thickened at 16 millimeters and with normal motion. Fractional shortening 43%. Aortic and mitral valve movements appeared normal. No evidence for valve insufficiency on color coding. No signs of valvular stenosis on pulsed Doppler.

Summary assessment: In our examination, there were now only very occasional ventricular extrasystoles during the exercise test. No worrisome rhythm disturbances were found. The rare Wenckebach-type atrioventricular block in the prior long-term ECG could well be of functional origin. I found no evidence of coronary artery disease at this time. The primary issue is hypertensive heart disease with almost 20 kg of excess weight, along with echocardiographic signs of beginning left ventricular hypertrophy and enlargement of the left atrium. I consider the ongoing medication with a low-dose beta blocker to be adequate. It is important that he quits smoking and reduces his excess weight. In addition, regular physical activity is recommended.

Therapy recommendation and procedure: Metoprolol 50 ½-0-½. Low-sodium and low-cholesterol 800-calorie diet to reduce excess weight of 16 kilograms. Regular exercise given excellent physical capacity. Next follow-up in 1 year.
