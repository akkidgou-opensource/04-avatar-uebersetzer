Course: Ms. Williams was referred to us with a suspected diagnosis of pancreatic head tumor. The contrast enhancement was not significantly higher than the rest of the pancreatic parenchyma; overall, the lesion measured 2.1 cm in diameter. Therefore, there was an approximately 2.1 cm mass at the lower border of the pancreatic head.

After optimization of her general condition, the aforementioned procedure was performed on 03/27/2023 under general anesthesia without complications. The postoperative course was very challenging and prolonged. Only on the 7th postoperative day were we able to transfer Ms. Williams from our Intensive Care Unit I03 to the general ward after cardio-respiratory stabilization. The inserted drains were removed on time. At all times, wound conditions were non-irritated. As the patient developed significant upper abdominal pain and recurrent nausea, we reduced her diet advancement and performed an upper GI contrast study on 04/10/2023.

Upper gastrointestinal series:
Normal swallowing process, unhindered transesophageal passage of contrast. Normal passage of contrast into the gastric remnant, which is significantly dilated and appears relatively taut. In an upright position, adequate contrast transition into the subsequent small bowel loop. No evidence of contrast extravasation or stenosis.

With new symptoms of heartburn, we increased oral pantoprazole to 2 x 40 mg and started 3 x 2 mL Motilium orally. This led to a slow improvement in symptoms. However, the patient was still unable to consume adequate amounts of fluids and nutrition, so we provided intravenous supplemental nutrition for a prolonged period.

Due to limited mobility, Ms. Williams received multiple times daily care from Physical Therapy. A postoperative hypokalemia was treated over several days with potassium tablets. On 04/21/2023 we were able to discharge Ms. Williams from our inpatient care largely symptom-free with non-irritated wound conditions. From a surgical perspective, based on the histology, there is no need for further therapy. Since Ms. Williams was still in a reduced general and nutritional state, a home health care service was arranged for her. At the time of discharge, C-reactive protein was still elevated (97.1 mg/L). Therefore, we prescribed Ciprobay 250 mg 2 x 1 for 2 weeks. We kindly ask for follow-up blood count monitoring (also in regard to the pronounced postoperative hypokalemia). Pain management, which had been initiated for the remaining wound pain, should also be tapered and discontinued over time.
